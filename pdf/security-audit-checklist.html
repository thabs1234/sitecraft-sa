Is your website leaking customer data?

Most small business websites in South Africa fail at least one of these 4 security checks. The scary part: most owners only find out after a customer complains — or after a fine.

POPIA — why this matters to you

Since July 2021, South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act requires every business that holds customer details (names, numbers, bookings, orders) to keep them safe. If a customer's info leaks from your website, the Information Regulator can fine you up to R10 million — and you're the one liable, not your web developer.

1Can you tell who's who? CHECK 1

Authentication. If your site takes bookings, orders or payments, it must verify that a person is who they say they are before letting them in — a password, an OTP, a Google/Apple login. A site where "anyone can be anyone" cannot protect anyone.

❓ Ask: Can a visitor access customer-only areas without logging in?

2Can a robot flood you? CHECK 2

Rate limiting. A bot can fire your contact form or booking page 500 times a minute — fake bookings, spam messages, even crashing your site. Secure sites limit how many requests one visitor can make in a short time.

❓ Ask: Could someone (or something) spam your form 100 times in one minute?

3Can one customer see another's data? CHECK 3

Row-level security. This is the big one — and it has a 5-second test you can run yourself:

The 5-second privacy test
  1. Log into your site as one customer (e.g. John). Copy the web address (URL) of John's page.
  2. Log out. Log in as a different customer (e.g. Nancy).
  3. Paste John's URL into the address bar.

🚨 If you can still see John's details while logged in as Nancy — your site is leaking customer data. Every customer on your site can see every other customer's info.

4Does the site trust strangers? CHECK 4

Server-side validation. Everything a visitor types into your site (name, phone, email) must be double-checked by the server before it's saved. A form that trusts whatever comes in is the most common way hackers get into a database.

❓ Ask: Does your site check the data it receives, or save it blindly?
